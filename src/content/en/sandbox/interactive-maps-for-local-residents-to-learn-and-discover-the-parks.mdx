A custom interactive map I built at Other Tomorrows for the **Emerald Necklace Conservancy**, the historic chain of parks that runs through Boston, from Charlesgate through the Back Bay Fens, the Riverway, and Jamaica Pond to the Arnold Arboretum and Franklin Park. The client wanted visitors to read the eight parks as one connected system, and to explore any of them from their phone.

I joined after the first round of design and took over the map. The interaction is the part I own. I reworked it around one problem: how to let someone move across the whole system, cross from one park to the next, and open the details of any place without getting lost. Most of my work lives in that layer: the movement, the states, and the moment-to-moment feel of exploring.

## How it works

<Dek>The whole system shows at once, so finding a place feels like exploring, not searching.</Dek>

The map opens on the full necklace, so the whole system is visible at once. Panning and zooming moves across it, tapping a park focuses it, and tapping a point of interest opens its details: the photo, hours, accessibility, and the story of the place. A filter panel narrows by park, facility, or activity. Smaller touches keep it closer to exploring than searching: a focused park fills with green, overlapping pins fan apart so none get buried, and the loading state reads _finding a sun-basking painted turtle_, one of the critters that actually live along the Necklace.

## The build

<Dek>One React component for Framer, wiring Mapbox, Airtable, and the team's design together.</Dek>

It is a custom React component that drops straight into the Conservancy's Framer site. Mapbox GL renders a style over a custom Emerald Necklace tileset, and the content lives in Airtable, pulled through a small Cloudflare Worker so no keys ever reach the browser. It is data-driven by convention: add a field named `Filter--Activities` in Airtable and an "Activities" filter appears, with no code change. To keep it fast and cheap, the billable Mapbox map does not load until the first interaction; before that it is a static image of the necklace. Built with Claude Code, matched to the design screen by screen.

## How it landed

<Dek>Live for the Conservancy now: fun to move through, and the necklace reads as one system.</Dek>

What I care about most is that it stays genuinely fun to move through while still landing the one idea that matters: the Emerald Necklace is a single, continuous system, not a scatter of parks. It is live for the Conservancy now, and the version here is my own.
